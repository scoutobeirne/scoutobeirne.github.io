Syllabus · Spring 2026

Reimagining Ownership in Business: From Employee Equity to Economic Democracy

Who should own the companies we build? This course explores emerging ownership models that are reshaping how businesses create and distribute value. From employee stock ownership plans (ESOPs) at billion-dollar companies to platform cooperatives challenging Big Tech, we examine practical strategies for broadening ownership beyond traditional shareholders.

Through case studies and guest practitioners, students analyze the financial, governance, and competitive implications of democratized ownership structures. Topics include employee ownership, shareholder activism, platform cooperatives and DAOs, community wealth building, foundation and steward ownership, and reparative approaches to the racial wealth gap. Most sessions feature a guest speaker putting these strategies to work in real organizations.

Designed for students interested in entrepreneurship, impact investing, corporate strategy, or economic development, the course provides tools to design ownership structures that align stakeholder interests, reduce wealth concentration, and build more resilient businesses.

Class 1

Dreaming of Just Futures: Taking ownership of "our" economy and the future we want to live in

What kind of economy do we want to live in and create?

Topics

  • The arc of the course: existing models, practitioners, and institutional pathways for changing "the economy," not just "capitalism"
  • Why we focus on the economy (not just firms or "stakeholder capitalism")
  • Moving from critique and diagnosis to vision
  • Imagining beyond the default career and institutional pathways
  • The intrapersonal and interpersonal work it takes to build different economies
  • Connecting an in-class visioning exercise to concrete practices and narratives
  • The language of care: "people want to be heard and respected"

Guest speakers

  • Taj James from Full Spectrum Labs and John-Lund Deloassa (Berkeley Haas MBA '26)
  • Niloufar Khonsari, author of The Future Is Collective: Effective Workplace Strategies for Building a Culture of Care

Optional pre-readings (~20 min)

  • On self-managing teams: an HBS case study of a high-functioning 3,000-person business and its governance system
  • On The Future Is Collective: an article that captures many of the book's ideas
  • Gar Alperovitz, Principles of a Pluralist Commonwealth: Introduction
  • Marjorie Kelly, Building an Economy for Life: The Great Ownership Transition
  • On salaries: a CEO who took a large pay cut and paid all 120 staff at least $70k a year. A complicated example, given later allegations against the same CEO.
Class 2

Employee Ownership Models: ESOPs, Worker Co-ops, Equity Grants

How do employees become owners, and what outcomes does shared ownership produce?

Topics

  • ESOP structure: tax advantages, succession planning, performance data
  • Worker cooperatives vs. ESOPs vs. profit-sharing vs. RSUs
  • Multiple dimensions of ownership outcomes: not just wealth and income, but also voice, autonomy, dignity, and workplace democracy
  • Case studies: W.L. Gore (no managers + ownership), New Belgium Brewery (ESOP exit), Publix Supermarkets
  • Financial modeling: does shared ownership reduce profits or increase them?
  • Low-wage sector considerations: can ownership improve worker well-being even when wealth accumulation is limited?

Guest speakers

  • David Levine, Eugene E. and Catherine M. Trefethen Chair in Business Administration and Chair of the Economic Analysis and Policy Group, UC Berkeley Haas School of Business
  • Melissa Hoover, Managing Director of Ownership Culture at Apis & Heritage Capital Partners and founding Executive Director of the Democracy at Work Institute

Optional readings (~30 min)

  • Apis & Heritage Capital Partners video on how they support employee ownership (7 min)
  • David Levine et al., "POWER Act Research Analysis and Final Report" (2025). Start with the executive summary; if you have time, see the discussion of the "free-rider" problem on pp. 108–110 of the full report.
  • "The Case for Employee Ownership" by Project Equity. Skim, paying attention to the sections "What is broad-based employee ownership?", "Problem", and "Solution".

Additional resources

  • All articles from the POWER Act study (David Levine et al., UC Berkeley Haas)
  • Minsun Ji (2025), Article 4: Case Studies of Worker-Owned Labor Contracting in Agriculture and Healthcare: California Harvesters, Inc. and AlliedUP
  • National Center for Employee Ownership (NCEO), ESOP basics
  • California Employee Ownership Hub: support for small businesses
Class 3

Shareholder Activism as Ownership Power

Can small shareholders change corporate behavior?

Topics

  • The shareholder proposal process (SEC Rule 14a-8)
  • Environmental, social, and governance (ESG) activism vs. profit-maximization activism
  • Case studies: Engine No. 1 (Exxon board takeover), As You Sow (plastics campaigns), institutional investor climate coalitions
  • The dark side: activist short-sellers, corporate raiders, and "shareholder democracy" rhetoric used for privatization

Guest speaker

  • Kelly Poole, As You Sow
    • Tools for individual investors to file proposals
    • Successes and roadblocks
    • Q&A: can this scale?

Readings (~15 min)

  • As You Sow's About Us and Theory of Change pages
  • As You Sow's Amazon shareholder proposal, the basis for the in-class exercise
  • Shareholder Democracy: an organization doing similar work to As You Sow with a specific climate lens
  • Fortune on the Engine No. 1 vs. Exxon vote: an example of a successful activist hedge fund campaign

Class 5

Long-Term Ownership Models: Community, Foundation, and Perpetual Purpose

How do we design ownership for centuries, not quarters, and who should benefit across generations?

Three model types

  • Community wealth building
    • Community land trusts, local investment funds, public banks
    • Municipal ownership
    • Focus: place-based, democratic governance
  • Enterprise foundation ownership and perpetual purpose trusts
    • The Scandinavian model (Novo Nordisk, Carlsberg, Ramboll), with a 150-year track record
    • Perpetual purpose trusts, including the Traditional Medicinals case study
    • Patagonia's 2022 Earth trust
    • Purpose Foundation steward ownership (Germany)
    • Governance: how foundation charters define "purpose" (Ramboll "exists for employees"; Patagonia "exists for Earth")
    • Sustainability research: does foundation ownership support stronger environmental performance than public or private ownership focused on short-term returns?
  • Exits to community
    • Selling to users, workers, or mission rather than acquirers
    • Connection to perpetual purpose trusts as a mechanism to prevent future "re-extraction"

Environmental and intergenerational framing

  • Central tension: these ownership models are explicitly designed to balance current stakeholder interests with future generations and non-human stakeholders
  • Contrast with Class 2: where employee ownership can get trapped in serving current workers at the expense of the planet or future workers, foundation ownership and perpetual trusts can encode a long-term environmental mission
  • Research question: does public corporation ownership (with diverse shareholders and regulatory oversight) actually outperform employee ownership on sustainability? What about foundation ownership?

Guest speaker

  • Jeremy Johnson, Traditional Medicinals

Readings (~20 min)

  • Overview: "What's Steward-Ownership?", Purpose Foundation. A short explainer of the two core principles (self-governance, and profits serve purpose), with examples including Zeiss, Novo Nordisk, Patagonia, and Bosch. (~3 min read, plus a 6 min intro video)
  • Deep-dive case: "The Patagonia Structure in the Context of Steward-Ownership", Purpose Foundation. How Patagonia's two-entity structure (Purpose Trust and Holdfast Collective) works, and how it borrows from the Nordic foundation model. (~12 min read)
  • Video: "Transforming Ownership to Create a Better Economy", Armin Steuernagel, TEDxZurich. Purpose Foundation's co-founder argues that companies should be "owned by the most able, not the highest bidder." (~20 min watch)
Class 6

Reparations and Ownership: Closing the Racial Wealth Gap

How do we repair histories of exclusion and dispossession from ownership?

Topics

  • The racial wealth gap: slavery, redlining, GI Bill exclusions, subprime targeting (historical ownership barriers)
  • Reparative ownership models: baby bonds (Darrick Hamilton), seed funding for Black-owned businesses, land back movements
  • Case studies: First Nations Development Institute, Detroit's "Buy Black" campaigns, Greenwood (Black-owned banking app)
  • Low-wage sectors and dignity outcomes: when ownership may not close wealth gaps but improves voice, autonomy, and workplace conditions (drawing on the POWER Act case studies from Class 2)
  • California state support for employee ownership in underserved communities
  • Tension: individual reparations vs. collective ownership structures

Organizations and scholars connected to this class

  • Sogorea Te' Land Trust: an urban Indigenous women-led land trust in the Bay Area
  • East Bay Permanent Real Estate Cooperative (EBPREC): a Black-led cooperative putting land and housing into permanent community ownership
  • Jovan Scott Lewis: UC Berkeley professor of geography who served on California's Reparations Task Force

Readings (~35 min)

  • Ta-Nehisi Coates, "The Case for Reparations" (2014)
  • First Nations Development Institute
  • Brookings, "Examining the Black-White Wealth Gap" (2020)
  • POWER Act study case studies: California state-commissioned research on low-wage sectors, with a focus on dignity and voice
  • California Employee Ownership Hub
  • Greenwood (Black-owned banking app)
